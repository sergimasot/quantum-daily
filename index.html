Brussels proposes a Quantum Act and a fund meant to steer up to €50 billion into quantum and AI

The draft law sets post-quantum deadlines, builds national hubs and restricts public quantum tenders to EU-controlled suppliers. No budget is attached to the €50 billion target yet, and industry wants purchase commitments written in.

European CommissionEuronews · Startup Fortune · Quantum Computing Report · The Next Web

On Wednesday the European Commission proposed a Quantum Act, a framework law intended to turn Europe’s quantum research into industrial capacity, together with a Sovereign Tech Equity Fund that it hopes will drive up to €50 billion of investment into quantum, AI and other strategic technologies (Euronews). The act would set up hubs and competence centres for small companies in each member state, and the Commission and national governments would monitor supply chains; nine of the 47 critical components the Commission identified have no EU supplier. It would also require member states to move high-risk systems such as critical infrastructure to post-quantum cryptography by 2030 and all other systems by 2035. The proposal now goes to the European Parliament and the Council.

The Commission argues that Europe’s shortfall is in private money. By its figures, EU quantum start-ups raised €1.1 billion in private investment in 2025, against €7 billion for US start-ups, while announced public funding stood at about €8.6 billion in the EU and €5.3 billion in the US (QCR). The fund would pool money from the Commission, the European Investment Bank and member states, try to attract up to €6 of private capital per public euro, and be running by 2028, when the next EU budget starts. In September Thomas Skordas of DG CNECT said that the act “is not about budget”, and he called for consolidation among Europe’s 78 quantum start-ups (The Next Web).

The European Quantum Industry Consortium welcomed the proposal but asked for procurement volumes in the text: €300–500 million of commitments within six months of entry into force and €7.7–12.3 billion over five years. Startup Fortune reads the draft more coolly. The Commission’s FAQ says that no fixed budget is allocated, Article 23 says only that the Union “may establish” the fund, and a recital points it at companies larger than scale-ups, so founders raising seed or Series A rounds gain little. Article 20 would limit public tenders for quantum products to Union-controlled suppliers and authorised partners. Euronews describes the rule as open to non-EU firms with a European-controlled presence, while The Next Web, as cited by Startup Fortune, reads it as excluding the European subsidiaries of US and Chinese groups.

The coverage we read does not say whether firms in associated countries such as Switzerland would count as Union-controlled. Further details on the fund are expected in February, according to reports cited by Startup Fortune. The next things to watch are how Parliament and the Council treat the procurement clause, whether the industry’s purchase figures make it into the text, and how much public money is committed once the 2028–2034 budget is negotiated.

Entanglement theory · AI

A preprint claims NPT bound entanglement exists, with an AI-found Werner state

I. Faisal (Boston University) · 2611.34921 26Thu 12:07

A single-author preprint on today’s listing claims to answer the oldest open question in entanglement distillation. Islam Faisal states that the Werner state ρ = (23I − 3 SWAP)/1448 on ℂ8 ⊗ ℂ8 has a negative partial transpose but is bound entangled: from any number of copies, no separable operation yields a two-qubit state with Bell fidelity above (1+√2)/4 ≈ 0.604 (arXiv:2611.34921). The Horodeckis asked in 1998 whether every NPT state can be distilled. In 2000 DiVincenzo, Shor, Smolin, Terhal and Thapliyal, and independently Dür, Cirac, Lewenstein and Bruß, conjectured that some cannot, and the earlier claimed proofs either had gaps or were withdrawn. If the result holds, it also confirms that distillable entanglement is neither additive nor convex. The paper’s disclosure says that models from OpenAI and Anthropic, with an agent-memory tool, helped find the state and write the text. The proof depends on one explicit matrix whose properties are checked by computation, and the author promises a version with verification code. We found no expert reaction by Thursday morning. Claude writes this paper, and Anthropic makes Claude.

Complexity · AI

OpenAI claims the matrix multiplication exponent is exactly 9/4

Interesting Engineering · Shtetl-Optimized · OpenAI

A day after the VP ≠ VNP papers, OpenAI posted a preprint to its mathematics repository claiming that ω = 9/4 over every field. It adds a matching lower bound, at least ⌈n9/4⌉/2 nonscalar gates for formal arithmetic circuits, to the upper bound OpenAI claimed in October (Interesting Engineering). The bound covers algebraic circuits, not bit-level algorithms, and comes with a Lean formalisation and a companion paper on Euclidean geometry in the asymptotic spectrum of tensors. Scott Aaronson reports a further OpenAI release that settles the linear-gate complexity of the Fourier transform at N(log N)1/2+o(1), with a matching lower bound, and says his working assumption is now that proofs coming directly from OpenAI or Anthropic are correct unless shown otherwise (Shtetl-Optimized). He adds that he has heard, from sources he considers reliable, of unreleased AI results in cryptanalysis, but declines to describe them until he sees details. No human referee has yet published a check of the lower bound.

Bound Entanglement with Negative Partial Transpose Exists

I. Faisal2611.3492126Thu 12:07●●● High confidence

Why here A short claimed answer to the field’s best-known open distillation question (Problem 4 on the Horodecki–Rudnicki–Życzkowski list), squarely in quant-ph; 24 places above its prior (25), which cannot see the question’s fame. A slow start like Liu’s QIP(2) paper on 24 November is the risk.

Whether every entangled state with a negative partial transpose can be distilled has been open since bound entanglement was discovered in 1998. Werner states became the test case: it is known that NPT bound entanglement exists if and only if some NPT Werner state is undistillable, and over the past year several groups proved two-copy undistillability, with three-, four- and five-copy claims following. Faisal proposes a complete answer with one explicit state, ρ = (23I − 3 SWAP)/1448 on ℂ8 ⊗ ℂ8, which is NPT, that is, ρ(8, −3/23) in the notation of the open-problems list.

The theorem is stronger than undistillability under LOCC. For every n, any separable operation that produces a two-qubit state from ρ⊗n with nonzero probability gives Bell fidelity at most (1+√2)/4 ≈ 0.604. This implies zero distillable entanglement, and it implies the usual rank-two projection criterion, since any NPT two-qubit output could be distilled. The proof runs through six lemmas. ρ⊗n is written as the image of one fixed vector under a product of two local maps; a single explicit matrix Q satisfies four single-copy conditions; those conditions yield a matrix inequality for every number of copies; and the inequality bounds an off-diagonal entry of any output by its diagonal entries. Only the verification of Q is computational.

Known consequences follow at once. Every NPT state is distillable with PPT-preserving operations and becomes LOCC-distillable when a suitable PPT state is added, so distillable entanglement is not additive (superactivation), and a flag argument of Shor, Smolin and Terhal shows that it is not convex either.

Readers should be careful on two counts. The paper is a 17 KB first version whose disclosure says that OpenAI and Anthropic models helped find the state and draft the text; a version with verification code is promised. And the whole argument rests on the matrix Q, which readers will want to check independently before treating the problem as closed. If it survives, a single state and a single certificate end a 28-year search.

Bound magic in multi-qubit systems

J. Kitzinger, J. Eisert, R. Kunjwal2611.3487239Thu 12:07●●● High confidence

Why here An eight-page stabilizer result from Eisert’s group answering one of the first questions asked in the resource theory of magic, the profile that has led listings since November; the model’s top paper.

Can every nonstabilizer state be distilled into pure magic? In odd dimensions the answer is no, because Gross’s discrete Wigner function plays the role that the partial transpose plays for entanglement: Wigner-positive nonstabilizer states cannot be distilled. Qubits have no Wigner function with the needed properties, and earlier qubit no-go results covered only finite protocols, exact purification, sets that are not closed under tensor products, or restricted operations.

Kitzinger, Eisert and Kunjwal define a multi-qubit set Bn that plays the role of the Wigner-positive states. It is closed under tensor products and stabilizer operations, contains the stabilizer polytope, and coincides with it for one and two qubits. Any nonstabilizer state in Bn is then bound magic, undistillable by stabilizer operations with any number of copies and rounds, and they construct explicit examples for every n ≥ 3. For fault tolerance this means some noisy magic states are useless as distillation inputs, so tighter criteria for distillability are needed, and the paper shares today’s front page with its entanglement analogue.

AC0 ⊄ BQAC0/qpoly via Sublinear-Degree Pauli Approximation

X. Sun, J. Wang2611.348037Thu 12:07●●○ Medium confidence

Why here A plainly titled circuit lower bound in the cs.CC cross-lists, where we have missed 30 top-ten papers since October; Medium because cross-lists start more slowly; near its prior (4).

QAC0, constant-depth quantum circuits with unbounded-fan-in Toffoli gates, is the quantum analogue of AC0, yet it is not even known to compute parity, and quantum advice could in principle help it a lot. Sun and Wang give an explicit P-uniform family of linear-size monotone depth-2 CNFs on which any QAC0 circuit with arbitrary quantum advice has only inverse-polynomial worst-case advantage over guessing, in the single-copy, single-output model.

The tool is an approximation theorem: the acceptance operator of any such circuit is close in operator norm to an advice-independent Hermitian operator of sublinear Pauli degree on a designated register, with the degree exponent independent of width. The same approximation gives two stretched-exponential bounds, e−mα for every fixed α in (0,1): QAC0 circuits cannot tell state 2-designs from Haar-random states, and states prepared from few non-zero inputs have almost no weight in a constant-energy band of an NLTS Hamiltonian. Low-degree Pauli approximation is the same structural fact that drives Pauli-propagation simulation.

Reducing T gates in product formulas with agent loops

L. Mantilla Calderón, J. F. Gonthier, A. Zook, K. Nakaji, T. L. Patti, A. Aspuru-Guzik2611.335098Thu 12:07●●○ Medium confidence

Why here A big-name AI-agent paper with concrete cost reductions, the profile of Google’s Lean-agent paper that led on 9 November, though our 6 November note warns that AI papers draw fewer quant-ph scites than talk; near its prior (3).

Aspuru-Guzik’s group and NVIDIA ran a coding-agent loop around Nemotron 3.5 Lightning to redesign product formulas for phase estimation. Five independent searches of 100 code mutations each optimised one shared protocol over 115 HamLib molecular Hamiltonians with 4 to 16 qubits, minimising T95, the total T count including repeated runs for 95% success, while requiring chemical accuracy on every training instance.

The best protocol, ELOTE, merges contributions to the same Pauli string, uses a first-order formula and sets synthesis precision per term from coefficient size, Pauli support and diagonal character. It cuts median T95 by 85.35% against a calibrated nested second-order baseline, stays chemically accurate on 76 unseen Hamiltonians with 18 to 22 qubits, and a reaction-energy variant saves a further 58.90%. Every number is checked classically, so this is a well-audited heuristic with no proven bound, and the instances stay far smaller than FeMoco.

When does a noisy Bell pair attain the Rains bound?

M. Barber, S. Pirandola2611.33317—scites●●○ Medium confidence

Why here A short refutation from a known capacities group, the short-and-plain profile that beat our long picks on four listings, and it lands on the same day as the NPT claim; 42 places above its prior (47), which weighs neither.

Two Bell-diagonal states with the same singlet fraction have the same Rains bound, and finite-block numerics had suggested that the bound is attained. Barber and Pirandola prove that, under PPT-preserving operations, the bound is attained if and only if one of the four Bell probabilities vanishes. Every mixed entangled isotropic state, in any finite dimension, therefore lies strictly below its Rains bound.

For channels it follows that the two-way quantum capacity of the qubit depolarising channel is strictly below its relative-entropy bound, so that bound cannot be the final answer for the most standard noise model. The abstract does not give the size of the gap, which is what anyone hoping to pin down the capacity will want next.

Neural Network Decoders For Qubit Loss

J. W. Staples, W. Fu, Y. Dong, J. D. Thompson2611.348913Thu 12:07●●○ Medium confidence

Why here A loss-aware decoder with a new fast sampler from Jeff Thompson’s group, which meets the 20 November condition that decoder papers carry a known name; near its prior (15).

Loss-resolving measurements make decoding much better on neutral atoms, but loss falls outside the stabilizer formalism, which has kept studies to the surface code. The authors train a convolutional decoder on samples from slim, a new sampler for Pauli noise plus loss that runs about as fast as the best pure-Pauli samplers. On the surface code it matches most-likely-error decoders at much lower latency. On the [[144,12,12]] gross code with walking syndrome extraction, below 1% physical error a pure loss model gives over 103 times lower logical error than pure Pauli noise at the same rate.

The asymptotic spectrum of tensors is connected

P. Vrana, J. Zuiddam2611.3466410Thu 12:07●○○ Low-Med confidence

Why here Answers a question of Strassen in the theory behind this week’s VP ≠ VNP and ω claims, but as a cs.CC cross-list it should start slowly, hence Low-Med; 43 places above its prior (50), which cannot see the topical link.

Vrana and Zuiddam prove that the asymptotic spectrum of complex tensors of every order k ≥ 3 is path-connected: every spectral point joins every one-leg-versus-rest flattening rank. So the spectrum evaluated at any fixed tensor is a closed interval, which answers Strassen’s question. Their interpolation method works for any suitably decomposable Strassen semiring with a slice-rank-like cover property, recovers Vrana’s 2019 interpolation for graphs and Shannon capacity, and simplifies results on rectangular matrix multiplication and quantum functionals.

Simultaneous computation of multiple excited states via double bracket flows

R. Aghaei Saem, M. Sahebi, Z. Holmes, Y. Suzuki2611.3487111Thu 12:07●○○ Low-Med confidence

Why here Holmes’s group at EPFL took an unpicked tie for third on 24 November, and our 25 November note gives its work a Low-Med floor; near its prior (16).

Double-bracket flows usually drive one state to the ground state. This paper uses one common flow to drive several pure initial states to several excited states at once, with no prior knowledge of the lower eigenstates. The authors give a necessary condition for convergence and a sufficient condition for exponential convergence, plus two schedules: Chebyshev scheduling, optimal near a fixed point but in need of spectral information, and an adaptive schedule that performs comparably in numerics without it.

All-electronic geometric phase gate on Doppler-cooled trapped ions using an axial mode

P. Rahimi, C. Knapp, P. Zantis, T. Maddock, S. J. Hile, J. Randall, M. Siegele-Brown, S. Weidt, W. K. Hensinger14final

Hensinger’s group at Sussex and Universal Quantum entangle two Doppler-cooled 171Yb+ ions, at mean phonon number 50, with an oscillating magnetic-gradient gate on the axial stretch mode of a surface trap. Exploiting SU(2) Symmetry for Efficient Quantum State Preparation of Matrix Product States

E. D’Argenlieu, A. Summer, F. Jamet, D. P. Tew, L. P. Lindoy13final

Phase estimation and real-time dynamics need a good initial state, and DMRG matrix product states can be compiled sequentially into fault-tolerant circuits. By the Wigner–Eckart theorem, only the reduced tensors need generic unitary synthesis, and the magnetic structure is restored by Clebsch–Gordan rotations with known angles. On Hubbard chains and ladders and on Anderson impurity models, this cuts Toffoli counts about threefold against U(1)-adapted compilation and by more than ten times against dense compilation. How to Read This Paper

the same in every edition

What this is

A weekday paper on quantum computing and the quant-ph arXiv. The front page covers the most important general news and what people are discussing online. Then come the day's eight papers most likely to draw attention, a check of the previous forecast, which subfields are busy, and industry news, conferences and deadlines.

It is compiled automatically by Claude, an AI model, from arXiv, SciRate, news sites, blogs and public forums. Summaries can contain mistakes, so read the source before citing it. Not affiliated with arXiv or SciRate. The public copy counts visits with GoatCounter, which sets no cookies and keeps no personal data.

When it comes out

Each weekday by about 08:30 Swiss time. arXiv announces new listings Sunday to Thursday at 20:00 US Eastern (about 02:00 in Switzerland), so each edition covers the listing announced overnight. Monday's covers Sunday night's listing, and weekend news. On Fridays the paper is updated around 10:30 with a review of the week.

What the ranking means

SciRate lets researchers "scite" papers they think are worth reading. After a day or two, the scite count is a good measure of how much attention a paper gets from the community.

At 8:00 the new papers have hardly any scites, so Today's Papers are ranked by a forecast of their final count. The forecast weighs the significance and novelty of the result, the track record of the authors and groups, how active the topic is, and lessons from comparing earlier forecasts with what happened. #1 is the paper we expect to be scited most. It is a forecast of attention, not a verdict on quality.

The forecast learns from its record. Every morning each past forecast is scored against the real SciRate ranking, a small statistical model trained on past listings (subfield, kind of claim, authors' past record) gives a second opinion on each new paper, and confidence tags are checked against how often they came true. Our record, under Yesterday, Settled, shows the average number of our top five that landed in the real top five, next to the model alone and to chance.

Confidence

  • ●●● High We expect it in the listing's top five.
  • ●●○ Medium Likely in the top ten.
  • ●○○ Low–Med / Low Plausible but uncertain: often a specialised or slow-burning paper we think deserves a look.

Each pick also says why it is ranked where it is.

Scite counts

  • 12early Count when the paper was written, a few hours after announcement. Unreliable.
  • 47final Count after about a day and a half, close to settled.
  • 31Tue 10:10 Count read from SciRate at that time during the day; the public paper is refreshed a few times a day (31live in the private edition, which updates instantly).
  • —scites No count yet.

Click a count to open the paper on SciRate.

Yesterday, Settled

Shown beside the papers, under SciRate today (today's ranking so far, with our predicted rank for each paper), so new counts update both. The previous listing's actual top papers by scites. We said is the rank we had forecast (— if we didn't rank it). The scorecard counts how many of our top five made the real top five and explains the misses. Worth a second look summarises high scorers we missed or underrated. If no counts are available yet, the section lists the previous picks instead (Yesterday's Picks, Revisited).

On Fridays, Last Week in Quantum does the same for the whole week, with the forecast record for each day.

Front page and The conversation

The lead story is the biggest general quantum story of the day, often the one people are arguing about. The conversation lists where the discussion happened: Hacker News threads, expert blogs, company posts, videos. We link to it and paraphrase it; a source being listed is not an endorsement.

Trending

Every new paper is sorted into subfields (error correction, algorithms, devices, many-body, sensing and so on) by keywords in its title and abstract, so a paper can count in more than one. The small chart shows each field's share of the last 15 listings; share, 5 listings compares the last five with the ten before (▲ up, ▼ down); scites/paper is the average SciRate attention from saved counts; top-10 slots counts how many of each saved listing's ten most-scited papers belong to the field, which shows where the standout papers are; cross-listed is the share of the field's recent papers that come from other arXiv categories, a sign of interest from outside quant-ph. Rising means the share grew by a quarter or more; Hot marks fields where the attention or the discussion is, whatever the volume.
