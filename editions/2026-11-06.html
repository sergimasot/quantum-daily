IonQ and Quantinuum raise their 2026 revenue guidance, but the two quarters are hard to compare

IonQ booked $173.8 million, about half of it from the SkyWater foundry it now owns. Quantinuum, in its first results season as a listed company, booked $9.1 million and pointed to its error-correction record instead.

IonQ, Quantinuum, IQMAssociated Press · company filings

IonQ reported third-quarter revenue of $173.8 million on 5 November, up 336% on a year earlier, and raised its 2026 guidance to $505–515 million. Remaining performance obligations more than doubled to $1.2 billion. GAAP net income of $564 million came from a noncash revaluation of warrants, and the adjusted EBITDA loss was $133.6 million. On the same day Quantinuum (Nasdaq: QNT) reported $9.1 million in revenue, up 233%, and raised its full-year range to $31–34 million from $28–32 million, according to its 8-K. It holds $1.94 billion in cash. The AP’s earnings summary gives a net loss of $17 million, which is the share attributable to Quantinuum Inc.; the consolidated loss was $118 million. IQM, now listed as IQMX, reported €8.86 million, up 51%, and a backlog of €102.5 million.

IonQ’s headline is mostly acquisitions. SkyWater Foundry Services contributed $83.5 million, about 48% of the total, and the IonQ Platform segment ($90.3 million, up 127%) also includes networking and security, the business Elisity joined this week. IonQ expects quantum-computing revenue to grow 110% this year but does not report it separately. It also reports reaching Stage C of DARPA’s Quantum Benchmarking Initiative (worth up to $300 million) and says NVIDIA will install a Superion 256 at its research centre. Quantinuum’s much smaller revenue came with technical claims. It says Helios reached 99.9995% logical fidelity in October, ten times better than its September record. Sol, targeting 100 logical qubits at a logical error rate of 10−5, is due in the second half of 2027, and Apollo in 2029. Its largest cloud contract to date, a multiyear extension with an unnamed global bank, includes committed access through Apollo.

Both companies beat analyst estimates. Zacks expected $8.2 million from Quantinuum, according to the AP, and analysts expected $141.6 million from IonQ, according to Benzinga. Market reaction reports disagree: Benzinga had IonQ up about 4% after hours, while Crypto Briefing reported a 10% fall on the day. The Quantum Insider (IonQ, Quantinuum) reported both quarters in detail. We found no comment from researchers. The 99.9995% figure comes from the company, and we did not find a preprint describing the October experiment.

Things to watch: Rigetti reports on 10 November. IonQ may or may not start breaking out quantum-computing revenue. Quantinuum has Sol’s integration slot in the first quarter of 2027 and a December Nexus release that adds mid-circuit dynamic branching. And a paper on the Helios logical-fidelity record would show which codes and which noise model the 99.9995% refers to.

Fault tolerance · Infrastructure

IBM, Oak Ridge and AMD put GPU decoders next to a Nighthawk r2 in Poughkeepsie

Quantum Computing Report

IBM, the Quantum Science Center at Oak Ridge National Laboratory and AMD have opened an error-correction co-design platform at IBM’s Poughkeepsie site. It pairs a 120-qubit Nighthawk r2 with ORNL’s Umbra cluster of AMD Instinct MI355X GPUs and EPYC CPUs, installed beside the dilution refrigerators so that syndrome data reaches GPU memory through AMD Versal SoCs with sub-millisecond round trips. The partners say this lets GPU decoders keep up with 100,000 circuits per second, which cloud links cannot. The first experiments will test hierarchical codes, an inner topological code inside modules with an outer code for correlated errors, and the first science targets are frustrated magnets compared against Spallation Neutron Source data. The stated targets, 100 logical qubits over 100,000 operations by 2028 under DOE’s Genesis Mission, are the partners’ own. The announcement gives no decoder latency or logical error rates. In the same week Yonsei University brought the first Nighthawk r2 outside the US online.

AI and physics · Verification

An improved Haldane-gap bound is withdrawn after a replay, as Grover extends the method behind OpenAI’s proof

GitHub · arXiv

OpenAI’s October preprints claim a proof that the periodic spin-one Heisenberg chain is gapped, from a “purity bootstrap” that turns finite-size partition-function data into a bound for all lengths. Volunteers have since built certificates for explicit gap constants. On 5 November the maintainer of one repository, which claimed γL > 0.1542 for every L ≥ 68 and so improved on an earlier community bound of 0.1515, added a notice that the claim is not certified. An independent replay had found four manual overrides of residuals, and without them the checker fails. The earlier certificate and the proof’s logic are untouched by this. In today’s listing Tarun Grover (UC San Diego) extends the bootstrap to higher dimensions, to near-degenerate ground spaces, to fermions and to tensor-network states (pick 5). His finite inputs are 4×4 and 8×8 calculations, and the paper does not say how they are certified, which is the step the GitHub episode exposed.

Certifying quantum code and circuit distances at scale

B. Cheng, N. Guo, F. Pan2611.08247—scites●●● High confidence

Why here A public exact solver that corrects published distances, including IBM’s two-gross circuit, in the QEC-tools category that has done best with us; the prior agrees.

Code and circuit distances feed into code comparisons, schedule choices and resource estimates, yet many published values are upper bounds from randomized searches, and the circuit distance under a given syndrome schedule is harder to pin down still. Cheng, Guo and Pan write both distances as tropical (min-plus) partition functions. They shrink the instance with circuit reductions and relaxations, then evaluate it with rank-based dynamic programming and a weight-bounded search. A proved lower bound together with an explicit logical error of the same weight certifies the exact value.

On their own 74-instance suite the method certifies 59 instances in 600 s on one thread, against 38 for all other tested solvers combined (dist-m4ri, Qubitserf, HiGHS, CP-SAT, Gurobi, SAT and MaxSAT solvers). On Webster, Jacob and Higgott’s independent 126-instance benchmark it certifies 95, against 73. The largest certified objects are a modular hyperbolic [[31200,1954,24]] code and a Cornucopia [[2304,1156,16]] memory circuit with about 8×105 fault mechanisms, whose circuit distance at eight cycles is 14.

The audit finds 22 overestimated values in 10 of 26 papers from 2023–26. Mitten codes [[200,40,12]], [[300,60,14]] and [[500,100,16]] have circuit distances 9, 12 and 9. A [[200,24,14]] routing code has circuit distance 10. IBM’s two-gross [[288,12,18]] memory circuit is certified at 16 in both bases under the standard schedule, which the architecture study assumes is 18. In 7,056 of 8,496 schedules a single family of hook errors caps the distance at 16. Redesigned schedules reach 18 at one noisy cycle, at the cost of one more timestep per cycle. At 99.98% junction yield, keeping blocks with dead checks that are certified at distance 10 or more raises the usable share of gross-code blocks from 53% to 94%.

Limits: for IBM’s [[756,16,34]] circuit only a lower bound of 15 is certified, and the cost is exponential in the worst case. The code is MIT-licensed.

Quantum Advantage with Sign-Free Proofs: An Oracle Separation between StoqMA and MA

Hamoudi, Le Borgne, Teganahally Sridhara (Bordeaux)2611.08180—scites●●● High confidence

Why here A clean oracle separation in the MA–QCMA–QMA chain, the kind of complexity result we have most often left too low; 23 places above the prior, which does not weigh the question’s standing.

StoqMA (Bravyi, Bessen and Terhal, 2006) lets the verifier run only a classical reversible circuit and then measure once in the Hadamard basis. It sits between MA and AM, and stoquastic ground-energy estimation is complete for it. Since Bravyi and coauthors showed that MA = StoqMA if StoqMA admits error reduction, it has been open whether this sign-free verifier gains anything over MA. Hamoudi, Le Borgne and Teganahally Sridhara give a classical oracle relative to which MA ⊊ StoqMA.

The separating task is Perron-eigenvalue estimation on sparse, high-girth graphs with 2Θ(n3log2n) vertices, accessed through a randomly relabelled adjacency list. YES graphs contain an n-regular base graph of polynomial girth (λmax ≥ n), and NO graphs are 2-degenerate graphs grown from at most 2√n seed vertices (λmax ≤ 4√n). The quantum verifier receives the top eigenvector and runs a Hadamard test with poly(n) queries, without phase estimation. For the classical lower bound, a verifier handed vertex names must descend self-similar trees to hidden leaves, and a bit-fixing lemma for random permutations reduces arbitrary witnesses to that case. Error reduction for StoqMA remains open.

Structured Quantum Hardness from Unstructured Assumptions

X. Liang, T. Yamakawa (NTT), C. F. Yung2611.07413—scites●●○ Medium confidence

Why here A quantum version of the Bennett–Gill question by a well-cited crypto-complexity group, the type of oracle result that took first place on Wednesday; 29 places above the prior.

Bennett and Gill asked whether NP ∩ coNP stays hard relative to a random oracle, and GIKKLS (STOC 2023) gave a conditional classical yes. Liang, Yamakawa and Yung prove the quantum analogue: if UP ⊄ BQP, then NPO ∩ coNPO ⊄ BQPO with probability 1 over a random oracle O. The quantum case matters because Shor’s algorithm breaks the number-theoretic problems that put cryptographic hardness into NP ∩ coNP, and lattices do not obviously supply such languages.

The construction pairs a UP witness relation with a zero-knowledge proof of membership in the quantum random-oracle model. It rests on a new notion, strong dense pseudorandom hash functions, and on the fact that a random function is one against quantum queries. A compressed, partially fixed oracle removes the reference string, and a quantum Bennett–Gill lemma turns high-probability hardness into an almost-sure separation. On the way they obtain an unbounded-prover post-quantum NIZK for NP in the QROM. A barrier result rules out fully black-box reductions of worst-case quantum hardness in NP ∩ coNP to injective one-way functions plus iO. Removing the UP assumption is left open.

Frustration-freeness is decidable but unprovable

N. Shiraishi (Tokyo)2611.07337—scites●●○ Medium confidence

Why here A short, plainly titled paradox from a known name on a basic many-body property, the profile that placed first and second on 2 November.

Shiraishi considers shift-invariant nearest-neighbour Hamiltonians on an L×L square lattice with open boundaries and algebraic matrix entries, and asks whether one is frustration-free at every size. Theorem 1 says that for each local dimension d there is a cutoff L(d), the same for every Hamiltonian, such that frustration-freeness at L(d)×L(d) implies it at all sizes. Frustration-freeness is a rank condition, the relevant determinants are polynomials in 2d4 variables, and Hilbert’s basis theorem reduces the infinite family of constraints to a finite one. The problem is therefore decidable for each d.

Theorem 3, assuming ZFC is consistent, gives an explicit Hamiltonian with commuting terms that is frustration-free but cannot be proved so in ZFC. Wang tiles encode a Turing machine that halts only if ZFC is inconsistent. The two results fit together because L(d) is uncomputable as a function of d and grows at least as fast as the busy-beaver function. Cubitt, Pérez-García and Wolf proved the spectral gap undecidable in the same class of inputs, and this paper shows that frustration-freeness behaves differently: decidable for each fixed d, yet with uncomputable cutoffs and individually unprovable instances. It is 8 + 12 pages.

5

Extensions of the Haldane gap proof: higher dimensions, near degeneracy, fermions and tensor-network states

T. Grover (UC San Diego)2611.07400—scites●●○ Medium confidence

Why here The first substantial physics follow-up to OpenAI’s Haldane-gap proof, from a well-known condensed-matter theorist, while AI-proof stories lead the conversation.

OpenAI’s preprints derive the spin-one Haldane gap from finite-size data. With a Hermitian spatial transfer matrix, the purity ratios Z(L,2β)/Z(L,β)2 and Z(2L,β)/Z(L,β)2 satisfy a squaring inequality, so their deviations from 1 shrink doubly exponentially as L and β double, and a finite calculation gives a gap bound in the thermodynamic limit. Grover extends this to any spatial dimension. His main new result uses the duality between symmetry generators and symmetry twists to bound the splitting of nearly degenerate low-energy states while keeping a gap above them. From 4×4 and 8×8 calculations he obtains parameter regimes where the 2+1D toric code in a field has four states split by at most e−cL and the 2+1D transverse-field Ising model has two split by e−cL2.

For fermions, a finite calculation establishes a symmetric-mass-generation phase in a π-flux bilayer model, and for Jian and Zaletel’s symmetric spin-one PEPS the bootstrap proves exponential decay of all connected correlations. The paper calls its cutoffs convenient rather than optimal and does not say how the floating-point inputs are certified.

Machine learning quantum data to accelerate quantum Monte Carlo

Jiang, …, Babbush, Huggins, Lee (Google Quantum AI and collaborators)2611.08151—scites●●○ Medium confidence

Why here A Google algorithms team with Willow data and a concrete scaling gain; hardware-backed chemistry has drawn fewer scites than theory, which keeps it at Medium.

Quantum-classical QMC uses a trial state prepared on hardware, but computing overlaps from the stored shadow measurements is a classical bottleneck. The authors compress the measurement data into machine-learned models that return the overlaps directly. They report chemically accurate results on up to 40 qubits of Willow data for hydrogen chains and iron–sulfur clusters, more than twice the scale of earlier hardware runs. In a companion paper the same day, Provazza and coauthors distil matchgate shadows into a transformer neural quantum state and report a 99% cut in classical cost.

Polynomial-time classical simulation of boson and fermion sampling under thermal loss

A. Angrisani, H. Mhiri2611.08444—scites●○○ Low-Med confidence

Why here Classical simulation of lossy boson sampling took third place on 21 October, and this one covers fermions too; 17 places above the prior, which misses that track record.

For Fock-state boson sampling, Gaussian boson sampling with uniform squeezing and fermion sampling with magic inputs, any fixed nonzero thermal or pure loss rate allows polynomial-time sampling to inverse-polynomial total-variation error, with high probability over Haar-random interferometers. Under a flatness assumption the simulation runs in quasipolynomial time without Haar randomness, and it runs in polynomial time at logarithmic depth with constant loss per layer. Near full input filling the noisy output stops depending on the interferometer. It is 83 pages.

How to Read This Paper

the same in every edition

What this is

A weekday paper on quantum computing and the quant-ph arXiv. The front page covers the most important general news and what people are discussing online. Then come the day's eight papers most likely to draw attention, a check of the previous forecast, which subfields are busy, and industry news, conferences and deadlines.

It is compiled automatically by Claude, an AI model, from arXiv, SciRate, news sites, blogs and public forums. Summaries can contain mistakes, so read the source before citing it. Not affiliated with arXiv or SciRate.

When it comes out

Each weekday by about 08:30 Swiss time. arXiv announces new listings Sunday to Thursday at 20:00 US Eastern (about 02:00 in Switzerland), so each edition covers the listing announced overnight. Monday's covers Sunday night's listing, and weekend news. On Fridays the paper is updated around 10:30 with a review of the week.

What the ranking means

SciRate lets researchers "scite" papers they think are worth reading. After a day or two, the scite count is a good measure of how much attention a paper gets from the community.

At 8:00 the new papers have hardly any scites, so Today's Papers are ranked by a forecast of their final count. The forecast weighs the significance and novelty of the result, the track record of the authors and groups, how active the topic is, and lessons from comparing earlier forecasts with what happened. #1 is the paper we expect to be scited most. It is a forecast of attention, not a verdict on quality.

The forecast learns from its record. Every morning each past forecast is scored against the real SciRate ranking, a small statistical model trained on past listings (subfield, kind of claim, authors' past record) gives a second opinion on each new paper, and confidence tags are checked against how often they came true. Our record, under Yesterday, Settled, shows the average number of our top five that landed in the real top five, next to the model alone and to chance.

Confidence

  • ●●● High We expect it in the listing's top five.
  • ●●○ Medium Likely in the top ten.
  • ●○○ Low–Med / Low Plausible but uncertain: often a specialised or slow-burning paper we think deserves a look.

Each pick also says why it is ranked where it is.

Scite counts

  • 12early Count when the paper was written, a few hours after announcement. Unreliable.
  • 47final Count after about a day and a half, close to settled.
  • 31Tue 10:10 Count read from SciRate at that time during the day; the public paper is refreshed a few times a day (31live in the private edition, which updates instantly).
  • —scites No count yet.

Click a count to open the paper on SciRate.

Yesterday, Settled

Shown beside the papers, under SciRate today (today's ranking so far, with our predicted rank for each paper), so new counts update both. The previous listing's actual top papers by scites. We said is the rank we had forecast (— if we didn't rank it). The scorecard counts how many of our top five made the real top five and explains the misses. Worth a second look summarises high scorers we missed or underrated. If no counts are available yet, the section lists the previous picks instead (Yesterday's Picks, Revisited).

On Fridays, Last Week in Quantum does the same for the whole week, with the forecast record for each day.

Front page and The conversation

The lead story is the biggest general quantum story of the day, often the one people are arguing about. The conversation lists where the discussion happened: Hacker News threads, expert blogs, company posts, videos. We link to it and paraphrase it; a source being listed is not an endorsement.

Trending

Every new paper is sorted into subfields (error correction, algorithms, devices, many-body, sensing and so on) by keywords in its title and abstract, so a paper can count in more than one. The small chart shows each field's share of the last 15 listings; share, 5 listings compares the last five with the ten before (▲ up, ▼ down); scites/paper is the average SciRate attention from saved counts; top-10 slots counts how many of each saved listing's ten most-scited papers belong to the field, which shows where the standout papers are; cross-listed is the share of the field's recent papers that come from other arXiv categories, a sign of interest from outside quant-ph. Rising means the share grew by a quarter or more; Hot marks fields where the attention or the discussion is, whatever the volume.

Industry, deadlines, conferences

Industry & Startups covers company and funding news that matters but is not front-page material; minor items become one-line briefs, and quiet days stay short.

Deadline watch counts down the days to submission and registration deadlines in the next eight weeks, showing each event's next deadline: filled red means less than a week, tinted less than a month. Conferences lists upcoming meetings; the list is checked every week.
