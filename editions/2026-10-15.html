Top pick · today’s listing

Fourteen hundred logical qubits per block, and they can compute

Deng’s group argues that ultra-high-rate codes keep most of their advantage once you ask them to run gates, not just store data.

Z. Lu, W. Li, Y. Ma, …, D.-L. DengarXiv:2610.1907358Fri 11:00●●● High confidence

Ultra-high-rate codes promise to store hundreds of logical qubits in a handful of physical ones. The standing objection is that individually addressing and computing on those logical qubits brings back the overhead you saved.

Deng’s group answers with the Cornucopia architecture, built around a [[2844,1426,18]] Cornucopia code. Each block is a self-contained universal logical processor, and blocks are linked by bridges into a modular machine. The code’s translation symmetry makes a small family of reusable gadgets possible, which together address 1,422 of the 1,426 logical qubits individually.

On top of the gadgets sit three universal instruction sets: basic, high-throughput and fixed-extractor. Excluding magic-state factories, a block with enough workspace for any basic-set instruction costs 4.1 physical qubits per logical qubit, against 3.0 for bare memory.

That ratio is the paper’s headline: the rate advantage largely survives the step from memory to computation. Factories and decoding latency are not yet in the budget and will decide whether this becomes a real blueprint.

The Rényi area-law conjecture is false below α = 1

T. Kuwahara · 2610.15199 39final

Finite-range, uniformly gapped Hamiltonians whose unique ground states have volume-law Rényi-α entropy for α < 1 in every dimension ≥ 2, while keeping a von Neumann area law. A sharp rank–error trade-off makes α = 1 the exact threshold. Relevant whenever Rényi bounds are used to justify tensor-network compressibility.

Logical CCZ state cultivation

Y. Hirasaki, J. Y. Lee2610.2059158Fri 11:00●●● High confidence

Why here Magic-state factories on qLDPC codes are the hottest fault-tolerance topic of 2026, and this one beats T-cultivation on cost.

Toffoli gates need logical CCZ states, and the usual route is to cultivate four |T⟩ states and convert them. Hirasaki and Lee prepare CCZ states directly on bivariate tricycle and bicycle (BT/BB) codes, exploiting the cup-product CCZ gates that BT codes support natively.

Two ingredients make it work, both built from projection-preserving lifts that engineer the logical coupling between BT and BB codes. Index-2 lifted BB ancillas allow selective Z-basis initialization, so one round of the native gates produces two disjoint logical CCZ states. Those states are then transferred into index-3 lifted BB memories with high distance, and decoder-based post-selection filters the output.

Exact simulations under circuit-level noise modelled on neutral atoms (0.01% single-qubit, 0.1% CZ, 0.5% CCZ) give joint infidelity 1.84×10−5 at 22% acceptance, or 4.1×10−7 at 9%. At the smallest factory's target fidelity, the spacetime cost per CCZ is below T-cultivation plus conversion.

3

Logical gate set tomography

Wysocki, …, Blume-Kohout, Rudinger (Sandia, Quantinuum)2610.1903443Fri 11:00●●○ Medium confidence

Why here Sandia–Quantinuum collaboration with a first-of-its-kind experiment; characterization papers from this team are widely read.

Knowing how much logical noise survives error correction is not enough to predict whether a computation succeeds; you also need its form. Logical gate set tomography (logical GST) extends GST to encoded operations, producing a full, self-consistent model of noisy logical gates from which fidelities and diamond distances follow, and testing for non-Markovian and coherent logical errors.

The team applies it to a universal single-qubit logical gate set in the seven-qubit Steane code on Quantinuum's 20-qubit H1 trapped-ion processor: the first such complete characterization. On hardware, errors are predominantly Pauli-stochastic, with no evidence of logical non-Markovianity and only weak hints of coherent error.

Higher-shot data from Quantinuum's detailed emulator does show weak but significant non-Markovianity and strong coherent logical error, and the authors use the emulator to attribute logical error mechanisms to specific physical error sources. Directly relevant to validating the noise models that detector-error-model decoders assume.

4

Testing graph isomorphism using simple quantum circuits

S. Beasley, A. Montanaro2610.1996223Fri 11:00●●○ Medium confidence

Why here Montanaro plus a classic open problem tends to draw readers, though numerical results usually score below proofs.

Can simple graph-dependent quantum circuits tell non-isomorphic graphs apart? Beasley and Montanaro study IQP and QAOA circuits built from the graph and first show that the graph itself can be reconstructed from polynomially many output samples.

Going beyond earlier work that used the QAOA energy, they find that Hamming-weight distributions separate graph pairs even at depths where the energy gap is zero. Applying graph invariants to the matrix of ZZ correlators works well: the spectrum at QAOA depth p = 2 separates all 10,486 cospectral regular pairs tested, up to 18 vertices. For IQP circuits, an invariant built from probability multiplicities within Hamming-weight sectors separates every non-isomorphic pair tested, and the output distribution has exact graph-theoretic characterizations at θ = π/2 and π/4.

The circuits clearly encode a lot of structure; whether it can be extracted efficiently for general graph isomorphism stays open.

5

Instability of Markov chains under infinitesimal coherent dissipation

Negari, Zou, Hsieh2610.1898316Fri 11:00●●○ Medium confidence

Why here Hsieh's group on mixed-state phases is an active and well-followed thread; a clean negative result travels well.

Conditional mutual information and its Markov length are central to recent definitions of mixed-state phases. Under local Lindbladian evolution, CMI growth is not bounded by Lieb–Robinson arguments, but how fast the Markov length can grow was unclear.

Negari, Zou and Hsieh show that an arbitrarily short, strictly local Lindbladian can produce infinite Markov length starting from a product state. They also construct strictly local Lindbladians that relax quickly to steady states with algebraically decaying CMI.

Despite those long-range conditional correlations, the states are locally reversibly connected to a product state by circuits of sublinear depth. The conclusion: infinite Markov length implies neither slow relaxation nor an obstruction to phase stability, suitably defined. Anyone using CMI as a phase diagnostic should read this.

6

The cost of classically simulating quantum communication is as high as it could be

Castellano, Sekatski, Brunner2610.1906012Fri 11:00●●○ Medium confidence

Why here Settles a long-standing foundations question from Brunner's group; such papers often climb slowly over several days.

How much classical communication does it take to simulate measurements on a d-dimensional quantum system? In the prepare-and-measure scenario, Castellano, Sekatski and Brunner answer from both sides.

Positively, they give a protocol that exactly simulates arbitrary d-dimensional states and arbitrary measurements with a finite average number of bits of one-way communication. Negatively, they prove that for d ≥ 4 no protocol with a finite average message dimension exists, strengthening a recent result of de Gois, Santos and Vieira.

The positive result follows from a more general simulation theorem that also covers some infinite-dimensional systems: multimode Gaussian states of bounded average energy with general Gaussian measurements admit exact simulation with a finite expected number of bits. It even extends to generalized probabilistic theories. The title's verdict: the classical cost of simulating quantum communication is essentially as high as it could be.

7

Gap-priced |T⟩ states

Poster, Sethi, Liu, Baker, Iancu2610.1929920Fri 11:00●●○ Medium confidence

Why here Practical compilation for magic states is in demand; architecture papers from systems groups get steady attention.

Magic-state preparation dominates the cost of fault-tolerant computing on surface codes, yet cultivation and distillation are usually provisioned statically, leaving much of a program's error budget unused. GP|T⟩-S is a lattice-surgery scheduler and factory allocator that co-optimizes both under a total T-error budget.

It uses each cultivated state's complementary gap as a fidelity estimate and adjusts the post-selection cutoff on the fly, turning budget slack into speed. It also catalogues distillation factories by size, production rate and slack, including new “cultivating factories” that embed cultivation inside routing channels and reach two-level-distillation fidelity at a single-level footprint. Dynamic programming then picks factories and cutoff to minimize spacetime volume, execution time or space.

Across 16 benchmarks at T-error budgets of 10−3 and 10−5, it gives a 5.5× geometric-mean reduction in logical cycles and 3.4× in spacetime volume, without exceeding the budget.

8

Invertible subalgebras for chiral semion topological order

Chen, Sun, Tantivasadakarn, Wang, Yang2610.1895310Fri 11:00●○○ Low-Med confidence

Why here Strong authors, but QCA and invertible-subalgebra papers serve a smaller audience.

Chiral topological order cannot be realized by strictly 2D commuting-projector Hamiltonians on tensor-product spaces; invertible subalgebras (ISAs) offer another route. The authors construct an ISA supporting chiral semions, and its anti-semion commutant, on a square lattice with one qubit per edge, with explicit non-Pauli generators, starting from the ℤ4 toric code and condensing e²m².

Pumping the semion ISA along z gives an explicit 3D non-Clifford QCA; eight pumps equal a translation followed by a finite-depth circuit. A similar construction gives a U(1)4 ISA, and together with earlier ones, stacking realizes every pointed Witt class of bosonic Abelian anyon theories. Both extend to dimensions d = 4k − 2.

Exponential advantage in stationary-signal sensing

S. Park, C. Oh25final

Learning the temporal statistics of one realization of a stationary classical signal. Two-mode-squeezed probes with protected idlers reach polynomial acquisition time, while any idler-free protocol that measures each time bin destructively needs exponential time. A learning-theoretic separation in a realistic continuous-monitoring setting.

Bell’s go theorem

Catani, van der Schaaf, Soares Barbosa18final

Replaces measure-space realism with “records”, sublocales of a point-free locale. Modularity fails across contexts, and locally causal record models then reproduce every CHSH value up to the algebraic maximum without fine-tuning. Provocative, which explains the scites.

How to Read This Paper

the same in every edition

What this is

A weekday paper on quantum computing and the quant-ph arXiv. The front page covers the most important general news and what people are discussing online. Then come the day's eight papers most likely to draw attention, a check of the previous forecast, which subfields are busy, and industry news, conferences and deadlines.

It is compiled automatically by Claude, an AI model, from arXiv, SciRate, news sites, blogs and public forums. Summaries can contain mistakes, so read the source before citing it. Not affiliated with arXiv or SciRate.

When it comes out

Each weekday by about 08:30 Swiss time. arXiv announces new listings Sunday to Thursday at 20:00 US Eastern (about 02:00 in Switzerland), so each edition covers the listing announced overnight. Monday's covers Sunday night's listing, and weekend news. On Fridays the paper is updated around 10:30 with a review of the week.

What the ranking means

SciRate lets researchers "scite" papers they think are worth reading. After a day or two, the scite count is a good measure of how much attention a paper gets from the community.

At 8:00 the new papers have hardly any scites, so Today's Papers are ranked by a forecast of their final count. The forecast weighs the significance and novelty of the result, the track record of the authors and groups, how active the topic is, and lessons from comparing earlier forecasts with what happened. #1 is the paper we expect to be scited most. It is a forecast of attention, not a verdict on quality.

The lead story is the biggest general quantum story of the day, often the one people are arguing about. The conversation lists where the discussion happened: Hacker News threads, expert blogs, company posts, videos. We link to it and paraphrase it; a source being listed is not an endorsement.
